# Import your LinkedIn history (optional)

LinkedIn lets you download your own data. If you share a small part of it, your briefs fit you better. This is optional, and takes about 10 minutes of your time. You have to wait for LinkedIn's email to be sent to you, but you can do it any time after setup.

## Why do it

- **Better comment angles and post ideas.** Claude learns what you already post and comment about, so ideas match your topics and do not repeat what you have said.
- **Faster coach and source suggestions.** The people and companies you already follow on LinkedIn can seed your coach list and your topics.

## What it cannot do

- It does not bring in other people's posts. LinkedIn only provides your own data, so it doesn't replace the job that the LinkedIn automation option has of reading posts from the coaches you follow.
- It does not show replies to your comments. LinkedIn's export lists what you wrote, not what others answered.
- It is a snapshot, so it goes stale. If you import, your career guidance brief reminds you once the import is over 90 days old.

## What you do

1. On a computer (not the mobile app), open LinkedIn and choose Me, then Settings & Privacy, then Data privacy, then Download your data. Direct link: https://www.linkedin.com/mypreferences/d/download-my-data
2. Choose "Want something in particular?" and tick only these, if they are listed: **Comments, Shares, Member Follows, Company Follows**. Do not choose the larger archive.
3. Wait for LinkedIn's email. Some files arrive in minutes and others within 48 hours. The download link expires after 72 hours.
4. Start a chat with Claude, attach those files, and send this message. Keep the whole link in it, because Claude opens links that you send it:

   > Please import my LinkedIn history for my weekly briefs. Open this file, read all of it word for word, and follow the part called "For Claude": https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jordan-sf/claude-personalized-industry-and-career-briefs-repo/HEAD/setup/linkedin-export.md

   If Claude cannot open the link, open the file on GitHub, click the copy icon above the text, and paste it into the chat after your request.

LinkedIn explains each category here: https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339364

## Privacy

- Share only the four categories above. The larger archive includes your messages, connections, login addresses and inferences or other data LinkedIn has about you. Do not upload those.
- Claude keeps a short summary in your Settings doc. It does not keep the raw files.
- Files you attach live in that chat in your Claude account. When you are done, you can delete the chat and the downloaded files from your computer.

## For Claude: how to run the import

1. Confirm the person wants to import. Say what it does in two sentences and show them the steps above in plain words.
2. Accept only Comments, Shares, Member Follows and Company Follows. If they attach anything else (messages, connections, logins, inferences, profile data or the large archive), do not use it or summarize it. Tell them why and ask them to download only the four categories.
3. Check the columns of the Comments file. If it holds only dates and links, skip it and tell the person. Use the text in Shares, and in Comments when it has text.
4. Write a summary of up to 8 bullets, in your own words: the topics the person posts and comments about, and how they frame their points. Leave out the names of other people and any other personal data.
5. From Member Follows and Company Follows, pick up to 5 coaches, writers or topics worth adding. Check with a normal web search that each one publishes public content in the last 30 days. Do not browse LinkedIn. Show the list, let the person approve, then add the approved coaches and sources to the Settings doc. Never store the full follow list.
6. In the Settings doc, fill in the "LinkedIn history" section: set "Last import" to today's date and write the summary. Change nothing else without the person's approval.
7. Tell the person what you saved, that you did not keep the raw files, and that they can say "refresh my LinkedIn history" to redo it. Do not turn on the LinkedIn automation option or ask about it.
